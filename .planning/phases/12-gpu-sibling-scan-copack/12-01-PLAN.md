<objective>
Wire spike-024's batch-sibling-scans co-pack into production. Replace the TWO separate
per-sibling resident scan launches + TWO readbacks (one blocking `read_one_unchecked` per
leaf-node ≈ 59 syncs/tree) with ONE co-packed 2-slot scan launch + ONE readback per split
(≈ 30 syncs/tree). Add (1) a 2-slot `#[cube(launch)]` scan kernel modelled on
`find_best_splits_fused_kernel`, (2) a `scan_resident_siblings` Backend method that runs it
from two resident Handles and decodes both siblings from one readback, and (3) a growth-loop
reorder in `find_best_splits` that defers the smaller-child scan past `subtract_resident` so
both Handles scan together — gated to the resident-scan-only path with a byte-unchanged
fall-back and an `LGBM_SIBLING_COPACK` override.

Purpose: spike-023 found ~59 scan-readback SYNCS/tree = ONE per leaf-node (both siblings of
every split scanned in two separate launches + readbacks). On the launch-bound APU regime the
fixed sync latency dominates (~44 µs/sync at small), so halving the sync count nearly halves
the scan launch+readback time (spike-024 isolated ~2.0×, bit-exact). The win is structural
(launch/readback floor), NOT a math change — each feature's sequential REVERSE+FORWARD scan is
UNCHANGED, only WHICH launch it runs in changes (NO spike-016 reorder).

Output: the new 2-slot kernel + its Handle launcher, the `scan_resident_siblings` backend
method (default error + CpuBackend delegate + RocmBackend one-launch impl), the gated
growth-loop reorder, and the `LGBM_SIBLING_COPACK` env override. Bit-exact by construction;
CPU anchor + wide build path + routing untouched.
</objective>
